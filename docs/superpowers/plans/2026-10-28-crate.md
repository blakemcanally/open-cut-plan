# The Crate, an Open-Top Box — Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Add a second design kind, the open-top box, with finish steps on every design, a KALLAX fit warning, and a
Crate starter that makes the crate the product owner built.

**Architecture:** The open-top box reuses `designGeometry`: its width and height are `{ outside, cells: 1 }` axes, and
its depth is the outside depth. A new `kind: "open-box"` field on a design selects it. The functions that make parts,
errors, hardware, checks, steps, and drawings branch on the kind, and the open-top code lives in its own files. The
`system` field of today is the fit setting: `kallax` turns the KALLAX fit check on, and `custom` turns it off.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, zod, Vitest, React, Testing Library, Playwright. `npm run check` runs lint, types, tests,
and the build.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-28-crate-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- All user-facing text (messages, step text, labels, docs) is in ASD-STE100 style: short sentences, active voice, one
  idea per sentence, as the text of today.
- The IKEA-name rules of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-27-design-starters-design.md` §3a apply: generic names
  ("Crate"); IKEA names only as "KALLAX-style …" or "Fits KALLAX …"; IKEA names in capitals; no brand assets.
- A shelf design of today (no `kind`, or `kind: "box"`) has the same parts, hardware, checks, and drawings as before.
  Its assembly steps are the same, plus its finish steps at the end when it has any.
- The format version becomes `1.11`. Old files read as before.
- No code comment restates the code. Match the comment density of the file.
- Each commit message ends with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`.

## Review Focus

1. Every example file in `examples/` still gives no `design-stale` warning and no `finish` step after the change (T2).
2. An open-top box whose walls leave no room inside (width or depth ≤ 2 × thickness, height ≤ thickness) gives the
   `design-too-small` error, and the estimate, the steps, and the drawings return null, not a throw (T3, T4).
3. A thicker or thinner material keeps the outside size of an open-top box and changes only the inside parts (T3).
4. A finish step that is empty or only spaces is never saved, from the editor or the CLI (T1 core helper, T6, T7).
5. A quantity of 2 doubles the parts and the hardware, and the first step says to build 2 (T3, T4).

### Task 3: The open-top box model: errors, parts, hardware, checks, and the fit warning

**Files:**
- Create: `packages/core/src/design/openBox.ts`
- Modify: `packages/core/src/design/errors.ts`, `parts.ts`, `hardware.ts`, `checks.ts`, `packages/core/src/index.ts`
- Test: `packages/core/test/design/openBox.test.ts`

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `designKind` (Task 1), `designGeometry`, `pocketHolesPerEnd`, `pocketScrew`, `KALLAX`,
  `KALLAX_CLEARANCE_MM`.
- Produces in `openBox.ts`:
  - `isOpenBox(design: Design): boolean`.
  - `openBoxErrors(design: Design, geometry: DesignGeometry, units: Units): PlanIssue[]`.
  - `openBoxParts(design: Design, geometry: DesignGeometry): Part[]` — ids `${id}-front`, `${id}-back`,
    `${id}-side`, `${id}-bottom`; names Front, Back, Side, Bottom; grain `"length"`; group and design as
    `buildDesignParts` sets them; quantities × `design.quantity`.
  - `interface OpenBoxHoles { sideEnd: number; bottomFrontBack: number; bottomSide: number; total: number }` and
    `openBoxPocketHoles(geometry: DesignGeometry, units: Units): OpenBoxHoles` for one unit.
  - `kallaxFitIssues(design: Design, geometry: DesignGeometry, project: Project): PlanIssue[]`.
- `buildDesignParts` returns `openBoxParts` for an open-top box. `designErrors`, `hardwareList`, and `checkDesigns`
  branch on `isOpenBox`.

Sizes, with W, H, D the outside width, height, depth and t the thickness:

| Part | Length | Width | Count per unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front | W | H | 1 |
| Back | W | H | 1 |
| Side | D − 2t | H | 2 |
| Bottom | W − 2t | D − 2t | 1 |

Pocket holes (all with `pocketHolesPerEnd`, on lengths in mm): `sideEnd` = per end of a side, from H; 4 ends.
`bottomFrontBack` = per front or back edge of the bottom, from W − 2t; 2 edges. `bottomSide` = per side edge of the
bottom, from D − 2t; 2 edges. `total` = 4 × sideEnd + 2 × bottomFrontBack + 2 × bottomSide.

Errors (`openBoxErrors`), each with the design ref:
- `design-open-box` for: a `back` ("Design "X" is an open-top box, so it has no back."), a `mount` ("… so it has no
  mount."), `combined` cells ("… so it has no combined cells."), an axis that is not `{ outside, cells: 1 }` ("Design
  "X" is an open-top box. Give its width and height as outside sizes with 1 cell."), and the system `eket` ("Design
  "X" is an open-top box. Use the system kallax or custom.").
- `design-too-small` when W − 2t, D − 2t, or H − t is ≤ `EPSILON`: "Design "X" is too small: the walls leave no room
  inside."
- The `pocket-thickness`, `design-too-large`, and `design-conflict` errors of today apply too.

Checks for an open-top box: `pocket-chart`, `nominal-thickness` (the box material only), `design-stale`, and
`kallax-fit`. Not `kallax-opening`, `kallax-depth`, `eket-grid`, `shelf-span`, `mount-system`, or the back.

`kallax-fit` (only for `system: "kallax"`): one warning for each size over its limit. The limits are
`335 − KALLAX_CLEARANCE_MM` = 333 mm for the width and the height, and `KALLAX.depth.mm` = 390 mm for the depth, in
project units. Text: `Design "X" is <over> too wide for a KALLAX-style cube: it is <W> wide, and the most that fits is
<limit>.` ("too tall … tall", "too deep … deep" for the others).

Hardware for an open-top box: the pocket-screw line (quantity `ceil(total × quantity × 11 / 10)`, the chart screw),
and the glue line. No back screws, legs, feet, rails, anti-tip fitting, or wall fixings.

- Produces: `AssemblyBoard` gains `{ kind: "front" }` (the open-top box uses `{ kind: "back" }` for its back wall).
  `assemblyBoardKey` handles it. `openBoxSteps(project: Project, design: Design): AssemblyStep[]` in `openBox.ts`;
  `assemblySteps` returns `[...openBoxSteps(...), ...finishSteps(design)]` for an open-top box.
- Produces: `openBoxElevationSvg(project, design): string` and
  `openBoxAssemblyDrawings(project, design, steps): AssemblyDrawing[]`; `designElevationSvg` and `assemblyDrawings`
  call them for an open-top box.

The steps, in order:
1. `drill`, boards the 2 sides and the bottom: the side holes (`sideEnd` in each end of the 2 sides, on the inside
   face) and the bottom holes (`bottomFrontBack` along its front and back edges, `bottomSide` along each side edge, on
   its underside), the stock thickness, and the jig setting, as the drill step of today says it. With a quantity
   above 1, the body starts with the "Build N of these." sentence of today.
2. `join`, boards front, back, and the 2 sides: put the sides between the front and the back, flush at the top and the
   bottom, with glue and the chart pocket screws.
3. `square`: the two diagonals of the top opening, each `√(W² + D²)`, as the square step of today says it.
4. `join`, board the bottom: put the bottom between the walls, flush with their lower edges, pocket holes down, with
   glue and pocket screws.

No mount, anchor, back, mark, or spacer step.

Drawings:
- **Front view** (`openBoxElevationSvg`, also the starter thumbnail): the front panel W × H in the design colour with
  the part name as its title, dashed lines for the inner faces of the sides (x = t and x = W − t) and the top face of
  the bottom (y = H − t), and the width, height, and depth texts as `designElevationSvg` shows them today.
- **Step drawings** (`openBoxAssemblyDrawings`): a **top view**, W × D: the back along the top edge, the front along
  the lower edge, the sides between them, and the bottom inside. The states (accent, design colour, grey outline), the
  square diagonals, and the description texts follow `assemblyDrawings`, with "Top view" for "Front view". A finish
  step shows the finished unit: "Top view of the finished unit."
